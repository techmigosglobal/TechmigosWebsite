Product development is a sequence of decisions, not a straight line from idea to code. The process should reduce uncertainty early, make progress visible and leave a reliable product behind.

## Discover the problem

Clarify the user, business outcome, current workaround and riskiest assumption. Review evidence and define what would make the first release useful. A focused [discovery workshop](/blog/product-discovery-workshop) creates alignment.

## Shape the experience

Map the core workflow, prototype the important states and test the language with representative users. Include accessibility, responsive behaviour, empty states and recovery before visual polish.

## Build in demonstrable slices

Use clear contracts, small milestones and regular demos. Design, frontend, backend and QA should share the same acceptance criteria. Make trade-offs explicit when scope changes.

## Verify the release

Test critical workflows, permissions, performance, devices and operational recovery. Write a release record with known issues, rollback steps and ownership. Our [QA service](/services#qa) supports this discipline.

## Learn after launch

Watch real usage, support questions and failures. Decide what to improve, measure or stop. A product process is healthy when the next decision is grounded in what happened, not only what was planned.
