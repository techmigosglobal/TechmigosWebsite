Cross-platform product design starts with one shared user problem, not one identical screen. Web, Android and iOS should feel related while still respecting how people navigate and input information on each surface.

## Share the product model

Keep terminology, permissions, content hierarchy and business rules consistent. This gives users continuity when they move between devices and gives teams a stable foundation for engineering.

## Respect platform conventions

Navigation, back behaviour, keyboard input, permissions and notifications have platform-specific expectations. Reuse the intent and component principles, not every pixel. A shared design system should make the right thing easy without flattening the platforms.

## Design states before polish

Include loading, empty, offline, permission, error and success states for every critical workflow. These states expose where the product model is incomplete long before visual refinement.

## Keep the backend coherent

One product needs consistent identity, data ownership and event semantics. API contracts should make platform differences explicit instead of hiding them in fragile client logic. Our [backend development service](/services#backend) can support that foundation.

## Test the hand-offs

Ask users to start on one surface and finish on another. Measure whether they understand what is synced, what is local and what needs attention. Cross-platform quality is felt in these transitions.
