Build-versus-buy decisions become clearer when you separate strategic differentiation from routine capability. A familiar tool is usually sensible for commodity work; custom software earns consideration when the workflow itself creates an advantage.

## Score the capability

Rate how unique the workflow is, how often it changes, how deeply it touches customers, how much data it handles and how costly a workaround is. Add switching cost, integration effort and vendor risk to the same page.

## Include the operating cost

Compare licence fees with implementation, training, support, upgrades, security review and exit planning. Custom software is not free after launch, and purchased software is not free to adapt.

## Test before committing

Prototype the one workflow that makes the decision important. Ask users to complete it with an existing tool and with a small custom concept. Their friction is better evidence than a theoretical feature matrix.

## Keep a reversible path

Document data ownership, export formats and integration boundaries. A decision is safer when the team can change direction without losing the business record.
