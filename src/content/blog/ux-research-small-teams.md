Small teams do not need a research department to learn from users. They need a repeatable habit of asking good questions, observing real work and changing the product when the evidence disagrees with the plan.

## Interview for behaviour

Ask what happened recently, what the person tried and what they did next. Avoid asking whether someone “likes” an idea. Behaviour and workarounds are stronger evidence than compliments.

## Observe the workflow

Watch a person complete the task in its normal environment. Note interruptions, hand-offs, terminology and unofficial tools. These details often explain why a theoretically simple flow feels difficult.

## Test a prototype early

Give people a realistic task and let them think aloud. Do not teach the interface while testing it. Record where they hesitate, what they misunderstand and what they expect to happen.

## Synthesize without theatre

Group observations, distinguish patterns from anecdotes and write decisions in plain language. Link each product change to the evidence that motivated it. Our [discovery workshops](/blog/product-discovery-workshop) provide a useful structure.
